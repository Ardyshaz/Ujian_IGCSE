# Preparation strategy for students and parents

Prepared: 15 November 2026. Status: original general educational guidance, not a clinically validated intervention or an official MARA study schedule. Relevant curricular evidence is in [01-cambridge-igcse.md](01-cambridge-igcse.md); assessment limitations are in [04-ukkm-and-selection-assessment.md](04-ukkm-and-selection-assessment.md).

## Learning progression

Start with a short untimed diagnostic across English, Mathematics and Science. Explain that the purpose is to choose useful practice, not predict admission. Look at explanations and first attempts, not percentages alone. Use small topic sets, review the underlying idea, then practise transfer to a different context. Introduce timing gradually after the learner understands how to navigate and save answers.

For English, combine grammar and vocabulary with complete passages. Ask the student to identify evidence for an inference and distinguish what a text says from what they assume. Include short writing with a self/parent rubric: purpose, meaning, organisation, vocabulary and sentence accuracy. Keep writing feedback separate from objective auto-scores.

For Mathematics, ask the student to restate the problem, mark relevant quantities, identify units and choose an operation before calculating. Use diagrams, estimation and an inverse check. Explain each step, including why an attractive wrong answer follows from a particular misconception. Include multi-step contexts without making wording unnecessarily complicated.

For Science, ask what is changed, what is measured and what stays the same. Use observations, tables and graphs, then ask for a conclusion supported by evidence. Separate a prediction from an observation. Keep investigations safe; avoid unsupervised chemicals/electricity experiments. This is instructional design guidance, not a statement of official paper components.

## Suggested flexible weekly routine

| Day | Suggested activity | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Short English grammar/vocabulary set | Explain one new word in an original sentence |
| Tuesday | Mathematics topic practice | Rework a mistake with units and steps |
| Wednesday | Read a passage and discuss evidence | Answer a short question in own words |
| Thursday | Science investigation/data questions | Explain variables and the evidence for a conclusion |
| Friday | Mixed short practice or Daily Challenge | Choose one topic to revisit |
| Weekend | Optional timed practice, rest and family discussion | Compare with the learner's own previous work, not other children |

Adjust session length to energy, schoolwork and support needs. The table is an example, not an obligation or proof of optimal outcomes. Avoid turning streak loss into punishment; breaks are normal.

## English–Malay academic and command-word support

Retain the original glossary terms and add age-appropriate examples. Suggested command explanations: “calculate” means find a numerical answer; “compare” asks for relevant similarities/differences; “describe” says what happens or what is observed; “explain” gives a reason; “predict” suggests an outcome from information; “conclude” states what the evidence supports. These are simplified learning prompts, not replacements for a syllabus's official command-word definitions. Check context and translations when authoring each entry.

## Test-taking habits

Read instructions before starting. Note whether there is a timer, which questions remain unanswered and which are marked for review. Move on from a difficult item and return if allowed. Check units, negatives and the actual question before submitting. The platform should show saved/pending submission states clearly, and an adult should help resolve technical problems without treating them as a child's failure.

On a real assessment day, use the invitation's rules for identity documents, arrival time, permitted equipment and access arrangements. This app does not establish those rules. Encourage adequate rest and preparation; do not use severe time pressure, shame or claims that a test determines a child's worth.

## Parent monitoring

Discuss one strength and one next step after a quiz. Use actual saved attempts and enough evidence before labelling a topic weak. Retakes and feedback-assisted practice are learning activities, so identify them when interpreting progress. Ask whether vocabulary, concept knowledge, reading load or interface difficulty caused a mistake. No practice category is an official Cambridge grade or a prediction of MRSM placement.
